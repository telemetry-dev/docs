Alerts check your telemetry every 5 minutes and tell you when something needs attention: model cost heading over budget, a trace that might be an agent loop, a burst of failed model or tool calls, an app that stops sending traces, a drop in the pass rate of an evaluator, or ingestion that is failing or running out of quota.

Every alert belongs to a project and goes to the project's email recipients, Slack, Discord, and webhooks. Select **Alerts** in the project sidebar. The page has three tabs: **Rules**, **History**, and **Destinations**.

**Quota running out** and **Data mix shift** can also be set for every project at once, because the quota is shared by the whole workspace. Open **Workspace settings**, select **Usage** in the settings sidebar, and go to the **Alerts** section. Turning one on or off there, or saving its limits, changes it in every project, and a new project starts with that setting. A project can still change its own on its **Rules** tab. Read [Quota and data mix alerts](#quota-and-data-mix-alerts).

Workspace owners and admins can create and change alerts. Other members can see the alerts but cannot change them. Alerts are available on every plan.

## Turn on an alert

The **Rules** tab lists every kind of alert, and the **Alerts** section of the workspace's **Usage** settings lists **Quota running out** and **Data mix shift**. Each row says in one sentence when the alert opens.

To turn an alert on, select its switch. It starts with the defaults listed below and checks within 5 minutes. **Monthly budget** has no default budget, so its switch opens the settings instead; enter a budget and select **Turn on**. **Eval score drop** has no default evaluator, so its switch also opens the settings. A project with no alerts other than **Quota running out** and **Data mix shift** also shows owners and admins **Turn on 6 alerts**, which turns on the [recommended alerts](#recommended-alerts) with their default limits. A project that already has other alerts and a destination, but no **Tool errors** alert, shows owners and admins **New: Tool errors**; select **Turn on Tool errors** to add it with its defaults and the project's email recipients. Adding it does not change the other alerts. Turning **Tool errors** off removes the prompt.

To change an alert's settings, select its row on the **Rules** tab, or its name in the **Alerts** section of the **Usage** settings. Edit the fields under **Alert when** and select **Save changes**. **Current data** runs the alert against your current telemetry and says whether it would alert now (**Would alert now**, **All clear now**, or **Needs more data**), with the measurement an email would report. It sends no email.

Turning the switch off stops checks and email but keeps the settings and history. To delete a project alert and its history, select **Remove** at the bottom of its panel, then **Remove alert**.

On the **Rules** tab, the **Status** column shows each alert's status:

- **Off**: the alert is not checking.
- **Checking**: the alert is on and has not been checked yet.
- **All clear**: nothing is wrong right now.
- **Needs more data**: the alert does not have enough history to decide yet. See [Needs more data](#needs-more-data).
- **Alerting**: something is wrong right now. Ongoing alerts also appear at the top of the project's **Alerts** page, each with a **View alert** button.

The **Last 7 days** column shows how many times the alert went off in the last 7 days.

### Recommended alerts

The recommended alerts are **Cost per trace jump**, **Model errors**, **Tool errors**, **Traffic stopped**, **Ingestion errors**, and **Missing prices**, with their default limits. **Monthly budget** and **Eval score drop** are not among them because they have no defaults. **Agent loop** is not either; see [Agent loop](#agent-loop).

You usually do not have to turn them on. telemetry.dev turns them on by itself for a new project once it sends traces, if that happens within 30 days of its creation. Projects created before November 7, 2026 got them the same way if they had sent traces in the 30 days before November 10, 2026, or within their retention period if that is shorter. Otherwise, select **Turn on 6 alerts**.

For these automatic alerts:

- A new project's creator is its email recipient from the start, so its alerts go to them.
- A project created before November 7, 2026 that gets the alerts and has no destination gets an owner of the workspace as its email recipient.
- A project whose traces come mostly from a coding agent integration, such as Cursor or opencode, gets none.
- A project that already has alerts of its own keeps them as they are and gets none, and so does a project in a workspace with fewer than 6 rules left under its [alert rule limit](#limits).

This happens once per project, so an alert you turn off or remove stays that way. If a project had any of the other recommended alerts on, **Traffic stopped** was also turned on for it, unless the workspace was at its rule limit.

## Email recipients

Every alert of a project emails the same list of addresses, which is on the **Destinations** tab. Select **Add destination** and enter an **Email address** to add one, or select **Remove** in an address's row menu. A change updates every alert of the project. When the project has no alerts yet, the list starts with your own address. Leave it empty to record alerts on the project's **Alerts** page without sending email.

Workspace members with a verified email get alerts right away. Any other address has to confirm first. When you add it, or when you turn on the project's first alert if none is on yet, it gets an email titled "Confirm alert emails from telemetry.dev". The recipient selects **Review and confirm**, then **Confirm alert emails**. The link expires after 7 days.

Until an address confirms, it gets no alert email, and its row shows **Not confirmed**. To ask again, for example after the link expired, select **Resend email**. It sends the email again to every address that has not confirmed. Addresses outside the workspace can unsubscribe from a link at the bottom of each alert email. To stop alerts for a member, remove their address from the list.

When telemetry.dev sends an email, it checks each address again. An address stops getting alerts when its owner leaves the workspace or unsubscribes.

### Weekly findings email

Early each week, telemetry.dev emails a summary of the week before, Monday to Sunday in UTC, for each project that had findings that week. A finding is an alert that opened, or a failed check: a fail score from a pass/fail LLM judge, code check, or rule on a trace, outside experiments. The email gives the counts of the week and lists the latest 3 findings, each with a link. A week without findings sends no email.

The email goes to the project's email recipients who are members of the workspace with a verified email. Addresses outside the workspace get alerts but not this email.

To stop it, select **Unsubscribe** at the bottom of the email, then **Unsubscribe** on the page that opens. This stops the weekly email of that project for your address only; its alerts still reach you. The same page has **Resubscribe** to undo it.

## Slack, Discord, and webhooks

A project's alerts can also post to a Slack channel, a Discord channel, or an HTTPS endpoint of your own. Every alert of the project goes to each of these destinations as well as to its email recipients.

To add one, owners and admins open the **Destinations** tab of the project's **Alerts** page, select **Add destination**, and choose a type:

- **Slack**: create an incoming webhook for the channel in Slack. Paste its **Webhook URL**, which starts with `https://hooks.slack.com/services/`, and enter the **Channel** name to show in the list.
- **Discord**: in the channel settings, open **Integrations**, then **Webhooks**, and create a webhook. Paste its **Webhook URL**, which starts with `https://discord.com/api/webhooks/`, and enter the **Channel** name.
- **Webhook**: enter the **URL** of your endpoint. It must use HTTPS and resolve to a public address. telemetry.dev then shows a signing secret that starts with `whsec_`. Copy it, because it is shown only once. See [Check a webhook signature](#check-a-webhook-signature).

Anyone who has a Slack or Discord webhook URL can post to its channel, so telemetry.dev stores the URLs and signing secrets encrypted and never shows them again. The list shows the channel name you entered. For a webhook it shows the host name with only the last 4 characters of its first part, the port, and the last 4 characters of the path, such as `https://…ooks.example.com/…a9f2` or `https://…k3x9.m.pipedream.net`, because some services put a token in the first part of the host name. An IP address, localhost, a host such as `example.com` that has no part before its domain, or a first part of 4 or fewer characters is shown whole, and a token in a later part of the host name still shows. To change a URL, remove the destination and add it again. To remove a destination, select **Remove** in its row menu. A project can have up to 10 Slack, Discord, and webhook destinations.

A Slack or Discord message has the alert title and the same sections as an [alert email](#alert-emails), with an **Open in telemetry.dev** link to the page to investigate and a **View alert** link to the alert's page. Messages mention nobody, even when telemetry quotes text such as `@everyone`.

### Webhook requests

Each notification is a `POST` request with a JSON body and the `content-type: application/json` and `user-agent: telemetry.dev-alerts/1` headers:

```json
{
  "type": "alert.opened",
  "reminder": false,
  "alert": {
    "id": "8f2c1a4e-3b7d-4c9a-9e61-0d5b2f7a8c31",
    "url": "https://telemetry.dev/o/org_4b1e/p/6a0c2d91-7f3e-4b8a-a5c2-1e9d4f6b8a20/alerts/8f2c1a4e-3b7d-4c9a-9e61-0d5b2f7a8c31",
    "title": "Possible agent loop in support-agent",
    "whatChanged": "One trace cost $3.82.",
    "comparedWith": "The limit is $2.00.",
    "whyItMatters": "It used 18 times the usual spend of a trace.",
    "likelyCause": "The search_orders tool ran 41 times in one turn.",
    "investigateUrl": "https://telemetry.dev/o/org_4b1e/p/6a0c2d91-7f3e-4b8a-a5c2-1e9d4f6b8a20/traces/4bf92f3577b34da6a3ce929d0e0e4736"
  }
}
```

| Field | Value |
| --- | --- |
| `type` | `alert.opened` when the alert opens and for each reminder, `alert.resolved` when it resolves. |
| `reminder` | `true` on the `alert.opened` that telemetry.dev sends again, at most every 6 hours, while the alert stays open. `false` otherwise. A destination added since, or one whose earlier request failed, can get a reminder as its first request for an alert. To open one incident per alert, open one for each new `alert.id`, and use `reminder` only to avoid paging again. |
| `alert.id` | The ID of the alert. Its reminders and its resolved notification have the same ID. |
| `alert.url` | The alert's page in telemetry.dev. |
| `alert.title` | The alert title. A reminder's title starts with `Reminder: `. |
| `alert.whatChanged`, `alert.comparedWith`, `alert.whyItMatters`, `alert.likelyCause` | The sections of the [alert email](#alert-emails). |
| `alert.investigateUrl` | The page to start from, the same as the **Investigate** button of the email. |

New fields can be added to the body, so ignore fields you do not know. Each request also has these headers:

| Header | Value |
| --- | --- |
| `webhook-id` | The ID of the delivery. It stays the same when telemetry.dev sends the delivery again, so you can use it to drop duplicates. |
| `webhook-timestamp` | When this request was sent, in Unix seconds. |
| `webhook-signature` | `v1,` followed by the base64 signature of the request. |

Answer with any `2xx` status within 10 seconds. telemetry.dev does not follow redirects.

### Check a webhook signature

The signature follows the [Standard Webhooks](https://www.standardwebhooks.com) specification, so its libraries can check it. For example, with the [`standardwebhooks`](https://www.npmjs.com/package/standardwebhooks) package for Node.js:

```ts
import { Webhook } from "standardwebhooks";

const webhook = new Webhook(process.env.ALERTS_SIGNING_SECRET); // whsec_...

// rawBody is the request body exactly as received, before JSON parsing. verify throws when the
// signature does not match or the timestamp is more than 5 minutes off.
const event = webhook.verify(rawBody, {
  "webhook-id": request.headers["webhook-id"],
  "webhook-timestamp": request.headers["webhook-timestamp"],
  "webhook-signature": request.headers["webhook-signature"],
});
```

To check the signature without a library:

1. Take the part of the signing secret after `whsec_` and decode it from base64. This is the key.
2. Compute the HMAC-SHA256 of `<webhook-id>.<webhook-timestamp>.<body>` with the key, where `<body>` is the raw request body.
3. Compare the result with the base64 value after `v1,` in `webhook-signature`, using a constant-time comparison. The header can hold several signatures separated by spaces; accept the request if one of them matches.
4. Reject the request if `webhook-timestamp` is more than 5 minutes away from your clock, so that a recorded request cannot be sent again later.

```ts
import { createHmac, timingSafeEqual } from "node:crypto";

function isFromTelemetryDev(secret: string, headers: Record<string, string>, rawBody: string) {
  const id = headers["webhook-id"];
  const timestamp = headers["webhook-timestamp"];

  if (Math.abs(Date.now() / 1000 - Number(timestamp)) > 5 * 60) return false;

  const key = Buffer.from(secret.slice("whsec_".length), "base64");
  const expected = createHmac("sha256", key).update(`${id}.${timestamp}.${rawBody}`).digest();

  return headers["webhook-signature"].split(" ").some((part) => {
    const [version, signature] = part.split(",");
    const received = Buffer.from(signature ?? "", "base64");

    return version === "v1" && received.length === expected.length && timingSafeEqual(received, expected);
  });
}
```

### Failed deliveries

When a request to Slack, Discord, or a webhook times out, cannot connect, or gets a `408`, `429`, or `5xx` answer, telemetry.dev sends it again after 1 minute, and then waits twice as long each time, up to 1 hour, for at most 8 tries. A `Retry-After` header can ask for a longer wait, up to 1 hour. Any other answer, such as `401`, `404`, or a redirect, fails the delivery at once.

Each Slack, Discord, and webhook destination gets its notifications one at a time, oldest first, so an alert's opening, reminders, and resolution arrive in order. While a delivery waits to be sent again, later notifications to the same destination wait behind it. A notification that is still not sent 24 hours after it was queued fails without another try, and the alert's **Timeline** shows it as failed with "Not sent within 24 hours of being queued", followed by the last error if an earlier try failed. Email is not limited this way, and one alert's email does not wait behind another's.

After 3 failed deliveries in a row, a Slack, Discord, or webhook destination shows **Failing** on the **Destinations** tab, with the last error, such as "Slack answered 404: no_service" or "The webhook did not answer within 10 seconds". A delivery that is retried counts once, when it fails for good. The next delivery that succeeds sets the destination back to **Active**. A notification that waited more than 24 hours, was too large to send, or that telemetry.dev could not process does not count.

The **Timeline** of an alert's page shows each delivery, for example "Slack message sent to #oncall", "Webhook to https://…ooks.example.com/…a9f2 is waiting to send", or "Discord message to #alerts failed". Rarely, a notification arrives twice; a webhook then gets the same `webhook-id` both times.

## Alert kinds

### Monthly budget

**Settings:** **Monthly budget** in USD, and the **Environment** to watch (default `production`).

Opens when the environment is on track to spend more than the budget this month. The projection adds the cost so far this month to the last 24 hours' spending rate carried to the end of the month, so a busy day moves it sooner than an average over the whole month would. Nothing is projected during the first 24 hours of a month. Months follow UTC.

For example, take a $500 budget in a 31-day month. By the end of the 10th, $200 is spent, $24 of it in the last 24 hours. The projection is $200 plus $1 an hour for the remaining 21 days, which is $704, so the alert opens.

When spending passes the budget, you get one more email saying so. It has no reminders. The projection also stops once the budget is passed, so an overspent month does not keep emailing you.

### Cost per trace jump

**Settings:** **Environment** (default `production`).

Opens when model cost per successful trace in the last hour is at least 1.5 times its usual level for the last 30 days, is outside its usual variation, and the increase adds at least $5 a day. When the hour has fewer than 100 successful traces, the rule uses the last 6 hours.

### Agent loop

**Settings:** **Cost limit per turn** in USD (default $2), and **Raise the limits to fit each agent's usual turns, or the project's while an agent is new** (off by default).

Not one of the [recommended alerts](#recommended-alerts), so it is on only when someone turns it on. Before November 10, 2026 it was one of them, so a project that got the recommended alerts before then may have it on.

A runaway turn is often an agent stuck in a loop, so the rule checks both cost and span count. A turn is a trace, or one agent span of a session trace with the spans below it. The rule opens when a turn that finished in the last hour costs more than the cost limit or has more spans than the span limit. Turns are grouped by agent.

| Limit | Raising the limits off | Raising the limits on |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Cost | **Cost limit per turn** | The higher of **Cost limit per turn** and 3 times the 99th percentile turn cost |
| Spans | 30 | The higher of 30 and 2 times the 99th percentile span count |

With raising the limits on, an agent with at least 100 turns in the 24 hours before the last hour gets percentiles from its own turns: from the last 6 of those hours when they hold 100 turns, and from all 24 otherwise. An agent with fewer turns uses the whole project's turns, counted the same way. The percentiles only raise the limits, for agents whose turns are usually large. They never add alerts.

### Model errors

**Settings:** **Environments**, separated by commas, and **Minimum calls** (default 50). Left empty, the default, **Environments** watches every environment except `development`, `dev`, `local`, and `experiment`. Rules saved with the earlier default, `production`, now use this one.

Opens when calls to one provider and model fail with the same error type often enough: at least 10 failures, a failure rate above 5%, and at least 3 times the usual rate. When the last hour has fewer calls than **Minimum calls**, the rule uses the last 6 hours.

A provider and model without enough history to have a usual rate, such as a model you just started using, opens when it has at least **Minimum calls** calls, at least 10 failures, and half or more of its calls fail.

**Investigate** opens the failed spans of the last 7 days for that provider, model, and operation in the project's traces page. A provider or model that was not recorded is left out of the filter.

### Tool errors

**Settings:** **Environments**, separated by commas, and **Minimum calls** (default 50). As for **Model errors**, an empty **Environments**, the default, watches every environment except `development`, `dev`, `local`, and `experiment`.

Watches `execute_tool` spans, grouped by tool name and error type. It counts failed tool calls even when the model calls or the overall trace succeed. If a span has no tool name, the rule uses its span name.

The rule needs at least **Minimum calls** calls to the tool. It opens when at least 10 fail with the same error type, the failure rate is above 5%, and that rate is at least 3 times its usual level. It uses the last completed hour, or the last 6 completed hours when the hour has too few calls. The comparison uses hourly samples from the preceding 30 days, excluding the hours being evaluated.

Without at least 7 comparable hours of history, the rule opens only when at least half of the tool's calls fail with the same error type, still subject to the call and failure minimums. A single failed tool call does not trigger it.

The email names the tool and error type and reports failed calls and affected traces. Its likely cause compares with the tool's earlier calls in the watched environments over the last 30 days:

- When the tool has no earlier calls, the tool may be new, so the email suggests checking its first failed calls for input or configuration errors.
- When the tool has earlier calls but none failed with this error type, the error is new for the tool, which usually points to a recent deploy or a change in the tool's input, permissions, or a service it calls.
- When the tool failed this way before, the email says how often and suggests checking whether a service the tool calls is degraded or rate limited.

Reminders repeat the likely cause of the opening email. **Investigate** opens that tool's failed spans of the last 7 days in the project's traces page.

### Traffic stopped

**Settings:** none. Each project's own history sets the limits.

Opens when a project with steady traffic stops sending spans, which often means the app stopped running: a crash, a failed deploy, a revoked API key, or a job that no longer runs. A project is steady when, in the 14 days before it went quiet, it never went more than 6 hours without spans, and it has at least 7 days of that history. The rule opens once no spans have arrived for twice the longest gap of those 14 days, and for at least 3 hours. For example, a project whose longest gap was 2 hours opens after 4 hours without spans, and one that sent spans every hour opens after 3. For a project that is not steady, the rule never opens: it shows **Needs more data** while the project is quiet, and **All clear** while it sends spans.

The rule counts the spans that ingestion accepted each hour, in every environment. It does not count these as quiet, either in the history or as a stop:

- Time in which ingestion refused the project's spans, such as when the workspace was over its [quota](#quota-running-out).
- Time in which telemetry.dev's ingestion was down.

A stop that began more than 7 days ago is not reported, so turning the rule on for a project that stopped long ago does not alert.

One hour of spans does not end a stop, because a stopped app can still send a retry or a run by hand. The alert resolves on the first check after spans arrive in two hours that are at most 7 hours apart, without waiting for three healthy checks. It sends no reminders. **Investigate** opens the project's [Issues](/platform/issues) page, which shows the same stop.

### Ingestion errors

**Settings:** **Minimum occurrences** (default 3, at most 100).

Opens when telemetry.dev records at least that many diagnostics with the same [diagnostic code](https://telemetry.dev/docs/reference/otlp#error-responses) within 5 minutes, from API keys for the same environment or from unscoped keys. It counts recorded diagnostics, not requests. Quota rejections are covered by the [Quota running out](#quota-running-out) alert instead.

### Missing prices

**Settings:** **Minimum calls** (default 100), and **Minimum priced share** in percent (default 90).

Opens when fewer than the minimum share of model calls with token usage have a cost. When the last hour has fewer calls than **Minimum calls**, the rule uses the last 6 hours. A call usually has no cost because telemetry.dev has no price for its model; see [Cost](https://telemetry.dev/docs/reference/cost).

### Eval score drop

**Settings:** the **Evaluator**, the **Environment** (default `production`), the lowest pass rate in percent (default 85), and **Minimum scored traces** (default 50).

Watches the scores of one pass or fail evaluator on traces. Read [Evaluators](/evals/evaluators). The rule counts the traces that started in the last 6 hours and got a score with a value from the evaluator. A trace fails when one of its scores from the evaluator is a fail. The scores of experiment items do not count.

The rule opens when the pass rate of these traces is less than the limit. With fewer scored traces than **Minimum scored traces**, the rule shows **Needs more data**. If someone deletes the evaluator, or changes it so that it no longer gives pass or fail scores of traces, the rule also shows **Needs more data** and tells you to select a different evaluator.

**Investigate** opens the traces of the last 7 days in the environment that have a fail score from the evaluator.

## Quota and data mix alerts

Every project has a **Quota running out** and a **Data mix shift** rule, which the **Alerts** section of the workspace's **Usage** settings turns on, turns off, and sets for all projects at once. Below it, **Earlier alerts** lists the workspace's quota alerts and the alerts of the workspace-wide rules from before each project had its own.

### Quota running out

**Settings:** **Warning: hours until the allowance is used** (default 72), and **Critical: hours until ingestion can stop** (default 24).

The rule uses the workspace's average ingestion rate over the last 6 hours. It opens when:

- The workspace is likely to use its monthly allowance within the warning number of hours.
- On the Free plan, ingestion is likely to reach the hard stop within the critical number of hours.
- On a paid plan, projected usage for the month is over 125% of the allowance.

It also emails once a month when usage passes 80% and 100% of the allowance, and right away when the ingest API rejects a batch because of the monthly cap or a gift budget. Rejection emails go out at most once every 5 minutes.

The quota is shared, so the quota rules of all projects share each alert: one quota problem opens one alert, not one per project. It usually goes to the destinations of the project whose ingestion grew the most in the last 6 hours compared with its usual rate, or, for a batch the ingest API rejected, of the project that sent it. If that project's quota rule is off or the project has no destination, another project with the rule on and a destination gets the alert, if there is one. An open quota alert does not move when another project's ingestion starts to grow faster. If the quota rule of the project that has an open quota alert is turned off or removed, the alert moves to the project with the oldest quota rule that is on, if there is one.

For plan allowances and the hard stop, see [Plans and billing](https://telemetry.dev/docs/platform/billing).

### Data mix shift

**Settings:** none. The rule compares with the project's history.

Opens when spans, log records, or metric points suddenly make up most of what the project sends. In the last hour, one of them has to be more than half of the project's records, at least 500 records, more than 3 times its usual count for that hour of the day, and a larger share than usual. The usual count and share are the medians of the same hour on the last 7 days.

## When an alert opens and resolves

Each check reports every rule as breached, healthy, or still needing more data.

<AlertLifecycleFigure
  title="How an alert opens and resolves"
  caption="Each dot is one check. The break skips ahead 6 hours, the time between reminders, so the strip is not to scale there. The alert opens on the second breached check in a row and resolves on the third healthy check in a row."
/>

- **Opening.** An alert opens after two breached checks in a row, about 10 minutes. Some opens are immediate: a runaway trace over the cost limit, a rejected batch, and ingestion errors whose HTTP status means a retry would fail too (anything other than `429`, `502`, `503`, and `504`).
- **Reminders.** While an alert stays open, recipients get a reminder every 6 hours. **Traffic stopped** sends none.
- **Resolving.** An alert resolves after three healthy checks in a row, about 15 minutes. **Traffic stopped** resolves on its first healthy check. Recipients get a resolved email if they got the opening email.
- **Quiet period.** For 30 minutes after an alert resolves, the same thing opening again still appears on the page, but its opening email is held. It goes out on the first check after the 30 minutes if the alert is still breached. An alert that resolves before then sends no email. The quiet period applies only to the thing that resolved: a different environment, model, or trace opening in that time is emailed as usual. Immediate opens are not held.

A rule can have several alerts open at once, one for each thing it watches: each environment for a budget, each provider and model for model failures, each tool and error type for tool failures, each diagnostic code and API key environment for ingestion errors. An **Eval score drop** rule watches one evaluator in one environment, and a **Traffic stopped** rule watches the whole project, so each has at most one open alert.

### Needs more data

Rules that compare with your usual traffic need history before they can decide. **Cost per trace jump**, **Model errors**, and **Tool errors** need at least 7 comparable hours from the last 30 days, and **Data mix shift** needs 5 samples of the same hour of the day from the last 7 days. **Missing prices** needs at least **Minimum calls** calls in the last 6 hours, **Eval score drop** needs at least **Minimum scored traces** scored traces in the last 6 hours, **Monthly budget** needs the first 24 hours of the month, and **Traffic stopped** needs 7 days of steady traffic; a project whose traffic is not steady keeps showing **Needs more data** while it is quiet. Until then the rule shows **Needs more data**, except that **Model errors** and **Tool errors** can open without history when half or more of the calls fail, subject to their count minimums. An existing model or tool failure alert can recover when fewer than 10 calls fail, including when traffic stops.

A rule with nothing to judge is **All clear**, not **Needs more data**. For example, **Model errors** is all clear when no calls are failing at all.

Checks leave out the last 3 minutes of data, so telemetry that arrives a little late is counted on the next check. The hourly rules use the last completed hour, so their results change about once an hour.

## Alert emails

Alert emails come from `telemetry.dev alerts <alerts@mail.telemetry.dev>`. The subject is the alert title, such as "Monthly cost budget at risk for production" or "Ingestion errors: invalid_json from the production key in Checkout". Reminders start with "Reminder:" and resolved alerts with "Resolved:".

Each email has these sections:

- **What changed**: the measurement that broke the rule.
- **Compared with**: the limit or usual level it was compared with.
- **Why it matters**: what the problem means for your application or bill.
- **Likely cause**: the model, provider, agent, or error that contributed most, when telemetry.dev can tell.

The **Investigate** button opens the page to start from: the project overview, the trace itself for a runaway trace, the failed spans of the model or tool for model and tool errors, the project's **Issues** page for stopped traffic, the project's **Setup** page for ingestion errors, the traces with a fail score of the evaluator for an eval score drop, or the workspace's **Usage** or **Billing** settings for quota. When the rule watches one environment, the spans are filtered to it. A name longer than 128 characters is left out of the filter.

## Alert history

The **History** tab of a project's **Alerts** page lists alerts newest first, 25 to a page. Select **All**, **Open**, or **Resolved** to filter them. Each row shows the alert, what happened, when it started, how long it lasted, and its status. Select **View alert** to open the alert's page, which shows **What happened**, the **Numbers** behind it, and a **Timeline**. Use the arrows below the table to see older alerts.

## Limits

| Limit | Value |
| --- | --- |
| Alert rules | 100 per workspace, counting the rules of all its projects except **Quota running out** and **Data mix shift** |
| Email recipients | 50 per project |
| Slack, Discord, and webhook destinations | 10 per project |
| Alert emails | 1,000 per workspace in a 24-hour window, including confirmation requests |
| Notifications per rule per check | 5 alerts |

Saving a rule over the alert rule limit fails with an error, and so does adding a 51st email address or an eleventh Slack, Discord, or webhook destination. The notification limits hold notifications back instead, and the alerts still appear in the history. A notification that the email limit holds back waits for every destination, including Slack, Discord, and webhooks. A held opening email goes out on a later check if the alert is still breached; an alert that recovers first resolves without email. An alert whose opening email was sent stays open until its resolved email can be sent.
