Alerts check your telemetry every 5 minutes and email you when something needs attention: model cost heading over budget, a trace that might be an agent loop, a burst of failed model calls, or ingestion that is failing or running out of quota.

There are two places to set them up:

- **Project alerts** cover cost, traces, model calls, and ingestion errors for one project. Select **Alerts** in the project sidebar. The page has three tabs: **Rules**, **History**, and **Destinations**.
- **Organization alerts** cover quota and the mix of data the whole organization sends. Open **Organization settings**, select **Usage** in the settings sidebar, and go to the **Alerts** section.

Organization owners and admins can create and change alerts. Other members can see the alerts but cannot change them. Alerts are available on every plan.

## Turn on an alert

The **Rules** tab lists every kind of project alert, and the organization's **Alerts** section lists the organization alerts. Each row says in one sentence when the alert opens.

To turn an alert on, select its switch. It starts with the defaults listed below and checks within 5 minutes. **Monthly budget** has no default budget, so its switch opens the settings instead; enter a budget and select **Turn on**. A project with no alerts also shows **Turn on 5 alerts**, which turns on five recommended alerts with their default limits.

To change an alert's settings, select its row on the **Rules** tab, or its name in the organization's **Alerts** section. Edit the fields under **Alert when** and select **Save changes**. **Current data** runs the alert against your current telemetry and says whether it would alert now (**Would alert now**, **All clear now**, or **Needs more data**), with the measurement an email would report. It sends no email.

Turning the switch off stops checks and email but keeps the settings and history. To delete a project alert and its history, select **Remove** at the bottom of its panel, then **Remove alert**.

On the **Rules** tab, the **Status** column shows each alert's status:

- **Off**: the alert is not checking.
- **Checking**: the alert is on and has not been checked yet.
- **All clear**: nothing is wrong right now.
- **Needs more data**: the alert does not have enough history to decide yet. See [Needs more data](#needs-more-data).
- **Alerting**: something is wrong right now. Ongoing alerts also appear at the top of the project's **Alerts** page, each with a **View alert** button.

The **Last 7 days** column shows how many times the alert went off in the last 7 days.

## Email recipients

Every alert on a page emails the same list of addresses. For a project, the list is on the **Destinations** tab. For the organization, owners and admins select **Destinations** in the **Alerts** section. Select **Add destination** and enter an **Email address** to add one, or select **Remove** in an address's row menu. A change updates every alert on the page. When the page has no alerts yet, the list starts with your own address. Leave it empty to record alerts on the page without sending email.

Organization members with a verified email get alerts right away. Any other address has to confirm first. When you add it, or when you turn on the page's first alert if none is on yet, it gets an email titled "Confirm alert emails from telemetry.dev". The recipient selects **Review and confirm**, then **Confirm alert emails**. The link expires after 7 days.

Until an address confirms, it gets no alert email, and its row shows **Not confirmed**. To ask again, for example after the link expired, select **Resend email**. It sends the email again to every address that has not confirmed. Addresses outside the organization can unsubscribe from a link at the bottom of each alert email. To stop alerts for a member, remove their address from the list.

When telemetry.dev sends an email, it checks each address again. An address stops getting alerts when its owner leaves the organization or unsubscribes.

## Project alerts

### Monthly budget

**Settings:** **Monthly budget** in USD, and the **Environment** to watch (default `production`).

Opens when the environment is on track to spend more than the budget this month. The projection adds the cost so far this month to the last 24 hours' spending rate carried to the end of the month, so a busy day moves it sooner than an average over the whole month would. Nothing is projected during the first 24 hours of a month. Months follow UTC.

For example, take a $500 budget in a 31-day month. By the end of the 10th, $200 is spent, $24 of it in the last 24 hours. The projection is $200 plus $1 an hour for the remaining 21 days, which is $704, so the alert opens.

When spending passes the budget, you get one more email saying so. It has no reminders. The projection also stops once the budget is passed, so an overspent month does not keep emailing you.

### Cost per trace jump

**Settings:** **Environment** (default `production`).

Opens when model cost per successful trace in the last hour is at least 1.5 times its usual level for the last 30 days, is outside its usual variation, and the increase adds at least $5 a day. When the hour has fewer than 100 successful traces, the rule uses the last 6 hours.

### Agent loop

**Settings:** **Cost limit per turn** in USD (default $2), and **Raise the limits when turns in this project are usually large** (on by default).

A runaway trace is often an agent stuck in a loop, so the rule checks both cost and span count. It opens when a trace that finished in the last hour costs more than the cost limit or has more spans than the span limit. Traces are grouped by agent.

| Limit | Raising the limits off | Raising the limits on |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Cost | **Cost limit per turn** | The higher of **Cost limit per turn** and 3 times the project's 99th percentile trace cost |
| Spans | 30 | The higher of 30 and 2 times the project's 99th percentile span count |

The baseline that raises the limits comes from the 6 hours before the last hour, or the 24 hours before it when the project had fewer than 100 traces. The baseline only raises the limits, for projects whose traces are usually large. It never adds alerts.

### Model errors

**Settings:** **Environments**, separated by commas (default `production`), and **Minimum calls** (default 50).

Opens when calls to one provider and model fail with the same error type often enough: at least 10 failures, a failure rate above 5%, and at least 3 times the usual rate. When the last hour has fewer calls than **Minimum calls**, the rule uses the last 6 hours.

A provider and model without enough history to have a usual rate, such as a model you just started using, opens when it has at least **Minimum calls** calls, at least 10 failures, and half or more of its calls fail.

### Ingestion errors

**Settings:** **Minimum occurrences** (default 3, at most 100).

Opens when telemetry.dev records at least that many diagnostics with the same [diagnostic code](https://telemetry.dev/docs/reference/otlp#error-responses) within 5 minutes, from API keys for the same environment or from unscoped keys. It counts recorded diagnostics, not requests. Quota rejections are covered by the organization's [Quota running out](#quota-running-out) alert instead.

### Missing prices

**Settings:** **Minimum calls** (default 100), and **Minimum priced share** in percent (default 90).

Opens when fewer than the minimum share of model calls with token usage have a cost. When the last hour has fewer calls than **Minimum calls**, the rule uses the last 6 hours. A call usually has no cost because telemetry.dev has no price for its model; see [Cost](https://telemetry.dev/docs/reference/cost).

## Organization alerts

### Quota running out

**Settings:** **Warning: hours until the allowance is used** (default 72), and **Critical: hours until ingestion can stop** (default 24).

The rule uses the organization's average ingestion rate over the last 6 hours. It opens when:

- The organization is likely to use its monthly allowance within the warning number of hours.
- On the Free plan, ingestion is likely to reach the hard stop within the critical number of hours.
- On a paid plan, projected usage for the month is over 125% of the allowance.

It also emails once a month when usage passes 80% and 100% of the allowance, and right away when the ingest API rejects a batch because of the monthly cap or a gift budget. Rejection emails go out at most once every 5 minutes. For plan allowances and the hard stop, see [Plans and billing](https://telemetry.dev/docs/platform/billing).

### Data mix shift

**Settings:** none. The rule compares with the organization's history.

Opens when spans, log records, or metric points suddenly make up most of the organization's ingestion. In the last hour, one of them has to be more than half of all records, at least 500 records, more than 3 times its usual count for that hour of the day, and a larger share than usual.

## When an alert opens and resolves

Each check reports every rule as breached, healthy, or still needing more data.

- **Opening.** An alert opens after two breached checks in a row, about 10 minutes. Some opens are immediate: a runaway trace over the cost limit, a rejected batch, and ingestion errors whose HTTP status means a retry would fail too (anything other than `429`, `502`, `503`, and `504`).
- **Reminders.** While an alert stays open, recipients get a reminder every 6 hours.
- **Resolving.** An alert resolves after three healthy checks in a row, about 15 minutes. Recipients get a resolved email if they got the opening email.
- **Quiet period.** For 30 minutes after an alert resolves, the same thing opening again still appears on the page, but its opening email is held. It goes out on the first check after the 30 minutes if the alert is still breached. An alert that resolves before then sends no email. The quiet period applies only to the thing that resolved: a different environment, model, or trace opening in that time is emailed as usual. Immediate opens are not held.

A rule can have several alerts open at once, one for each thing it watches: each environment for a budget, each provider and model for failures, each diagnostic code and API key environment for ingestion errors.

### Needs more data

Rules that compare with your usual traffic need history before they can decide. **Cost per trace jump** and **Model errors** need at least 7 comparable hours from the last 30 days, and **Data mix shift** needs 7 samples of the same hour of the day. **Missing prices** needs at least **Minimum calls** calls in the last 6 hours, and **Monthly budget** needs the first 24 hours of the month. Until then the rule shows **Needs more data** and does not open or resolve alerts, except that **Model errors** still opens for a model without history when half or more of its calls fail.

A rule with nothing to judge is **All clear**, not **Needs more data**. For example, **Model errors** is all clear when no calls are failing at all.

Checks leave out the last 3 minutes of data, so telemetry that arrives a little late is counted on the next check. The hourly rules use the last completed hour, so their results change about once an hour.

## Alert emails

Alert emails come from `telemetry.dev alerts <alerts@mail.telemetry.dev>`. The subject is the alert title, such as "Monthly cost budget at risk for production" or "Ingestion errors: invalid_json from the production key in Checkout". Reminders start with "Reminder:" and resolved alerts with "Resolved:".

Each email has these sections:

- **What changed**: the measurement that broke the rule.
- **Compared with**: the limit or usual level it was compared with.
- **Why it matters**: what the problem means for your application or bill.
- **Likely cause**: the model, provider, agent, or error that contributed most, when telemetry.dev can tell.

The **Investigate** button opens the page to start from: the project overview, the trace itself for a runaway trace, the project's **Setup** page for ingestion errors, or the organization's **Usage** or **Billing** settings for quota.

## Alert history

The **History** tab of a project's **Alerts** page lists alerts newest first, 25 to a page. Select **All**, **Open**, or **Resolved** to filter them. Each row shows the alert, what happened, when it started, how long it lasted, and its status. Select **View alert** to open the alert's page, which shows **What happened**, the **Numbers** behind it, and a **Timeline**. Use the arrows below the table to see older alerts.

## Limits

| Limit | Value |
| --- | --- |
| Alert rules | 100 per organization, counting organization and project rules |
| Recipients | 50 per rule |
| Alert emails | 1,000 per organization in a 24-hour window, including confirmation requests |
| Emails per rule per check | 5 alerts |

Saving a rule over the first two limits fails with an error. The email limits hold email back instead, and the alerts still appear in the history. A held opening email goes out on a later check if the alert is still breached; an alert that recovers first resolves without email. An alert whose opening email was sent stays open until its resolved email can be sent.
